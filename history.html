History


In 1966, plans to construct the Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant were first created during the development of nuclear energy production in the Soviet Union.

The power plant was built at its location in northern Ukraine due to several of geological and economic reasons.

A big reason was that it sat adjacent to the Pripyat River. A nuclear reactor requires huge, continuous amounts of water to manage heat and cooling systems. It also mainly supplied water for the 22.9 km ² man-made cooling pond/reservoir that sat beside the power plant. It also sat at a good distance to major energy-consuming cities, like the capital city of Kyiv (which is about 100km south from the Chernobyl npp in a straight line).



There were multiple factors that motivated the construction of the power plant. Mainly, it was to meet the rapidly expanding electrical demands of the central energy region, which included Kyiv and multiple other highly populated regions in the Ukrainian SSR, and prevent an electricity deficit. The Soviet Union also wanted to diversify its energy sources/energy grid, as they did not want to only rely on fossil fuels, leading them to lean more towards nuclear energy.

The plant was meant to be one of four that were going to be built around Ukraine. It was supposed to have twelve reactor units, and be built in six stages, making it the largest nuclear power plant if the original plans had been followed.

The power plant units themselves were RMBK-1000 reactors. The selected RMBK design was considered unusual as it prioritized cost efficiency and rapid construction over safety when compared to other designs. It was designed over the course of 1964-1966, and the project was led by Nikolay Dollezhal, the director of the NIKIET design institute with it being scientifically supervised by the Kurchatov Institute of Atomic Energy and therefore Anatoly Aleksandrov, as he was the head of Kurchatov Institute. Each reactor was capable of producing up to 1,000 megawatts of electric power and the four together produced about 10% of Ukraines electricity (at the time).

Originally, it was named The Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant of V. I. Lenin, after the founding leader of the Soviet Union and was proudly seen as a symbol of the communist technological prowess.

Like most other sites that held RBMK reactors, the construction of the plant was accompanied by the construction of a nearby city to house the workers and their families.

The city in this case, was named Pripyat.
